# F — Motorsports Pyramid: Shape, Economics, Geography

Research brief for an OOTP-style racing career management sim. Research done October 2026 with web search. Every factual claim has a source tag like `[S12]` that points to the **Sources** list at the end.

**Confidence labels:**
- No label: the number is stated directly by the cited source.
- **(approx.)**: a range, an older figure, or a figure from a secondary or blog source.
- **(unverified)**: from a forum or fan wiki, a single anecdote, or my own estimate. Use it only for game tuning.

## 1. Population and pyramid sizes

### 1.1 Number of tracks (US)

| Metric | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| All US oval tracks, drag strips, and road courses | **1,262** | 2013 National Speedway Directory, quoted on Wikipedia [S1] (approx.: the data is 13 years old) |
| Oval tracks | **901** | Same source [S1] |
| Dirt tracks, all types | **683** | Same source [S1]. A separate Wikipedia page says "over 700 dirt oval tracks in operation" [S2] |
| Active dirt tracks tracked by dirtfan.com | **583** | [S3] (approx.: this is one aggregator's count) |
| Circle tracks, dirt and asphalt combined | "nearly 1,000" | endurotrader listing [S4] (approx.) |
| IMCA-sanctioned dirt tracks | **240+** tracks, ~600 sanctions a year | [S5] |
| NASCAR Weekly Series (now "NASCAR Local Racing Series," since 2026) member tracks | **~50+** | US and Canada, 1/4-mile to 5/8-mile [S6] |
| Quarter-midget tracks | **50+** | [S7] |

**Takeaway:** Roughly **900 ovals** run in the US. About **75% are dirt (~650-700)** and about **25% are asphalt (~200-250)**. Most of them run a weekly program from about April to September. Dirt racing is "the single most common form of auto racing in the United States" [S2]. Common oval lengths are 1/2, 3/8, 1/3, 1/4, and 1/8 mile [S1].

- IMCA alone accounts for about 10,000 licensed dirt drivers.

### 1.4 Promotion flow and attrition

- **Cup rookies:** only **2** in 2025 (Shane van Gisbergen and Riley Herbst) [S23]. Cup normally takes about 2-4 new full-timers a year (approx.).
- **O'Reilly/Xfinity rookies:** about **8+** a year [S23].
- **IndyCar:** 25 charters and a ~27-car field means about **2-4 rookies a year** (approx.).
- **Indy NXT scholarship gap:** the USF Pro 2000 champion's scholarship ($546,500) covers "less than half" of an Indy NXT seat [S35]. Money, not results, is the main promotion bottleneck in open-wheel.
- **Rough funnel, my estimate from the counts above (unverified):**
  - About 60,000 short-track and dirt racers.
  - → about 1,500-2,500 regional touring regulars (CARS, ASA/Midwest Tour, SRL, NWSLMS, USMTS, ASCS regions, POWRi, USAC regionals).
  - → about 300-400 national touring or development pros (ARCA, Trucks, O'Reilly, WoO, High Limit, Lucas, USAC National, Road to Indy, IMSA).
  - → about 3-6 new Cup or IndyCar full-timers a year.
  - So the chance of a grassroots racer ever reaching Cup or IndyCar is about **1 in 10,000-20,000**. For a regional touring regular it is about **1 in 300-500**.

### 2.3 Sponsorship realities

**Cup primary sponsorship [S69][S70]:**
- Per race: about **$125,000-500,000**. Midfield teams charge $150,000-300,000; top teams $300,000-500,000.
- Full season: **$5M-35M**. Midfield teams cluster around $13-19M.

**Other NASCAR series [S70]:**
- **Cup associate:** about $200,000-1.5M a season.
- **O'Reilly primary:** about $250,000-1.5M a season.
- **Truck primary:** about $75,000-300,000 a season. Truck per-race associate deals run $15,000-40,000.

Rule of thumb: sponsors spend about **$2 on activation for every $1 of rights** [S70].

**Grassroots sponsorship:** this is mainly local small businesses. The Wall Street Journal has called local dirt-track success an economic indicator because "small businesses use sponsorships for marketing" [S2]. Typical local deals are product, parts, or $500-10,000 in cash (unverified). Most local racers are self-funded or family-funded.

## 3. Geography: regional ecosystems

### 3.1 Industry hubs

**Charlotte, NC ("NASCAR Valley")**
- About **90% of Cup teams** are based around Charlotte [S71].
- The region has 60% of North Carolina's racing employment and 70% of its revenue, and about 425 motorsports companies (2006) [S71].
- NC motorsports supported **27,252 jobs** and $5.9B in output in 2005, at an average salary of $71,000. NASCAR teams alone provided 4,559 direct jobs [S71].
- **Mooresville ("Race City USA")** has 100+ teams and racing businesses, and 183 racing establishments by mailing address. Penske, JR Motorsports, Spire, and Front Row are based there [S72].

**Indianapolis, IN**
- Indiana has 500+ motorsports firms and **23,000+ jobs**, at an average wage of about $63,000 [S73].
- Indianapolis Motor Speedway alone generates $1.058B a year, and the May events account for $566M of that [S73].
- Most IndyCar teams, the Road to Indy, and USAC are based in the Indianapolis area. Indianapolis, Charlotte, and England are called "the three leading motorsports economies" [S73].

**IMSA**
- Headquarters are in Daytona Beach, at the International Motorsports Center with NASCAR [S74].
- Teams are spread out: Indianapolis area (Wayne Taylor Racing with Andretti in Brownsburg, Era Motorsport in Westfield), Charlotte, and Georgia near Road Atlanta (approx.) [S74].

### 3.2 US regions

**Wisconsin / Upper Midwest (asphalt late-model belt)**
- Key tracks: Slinger Super Speedway, a 1/4-mile oval with 33° banking billed as the "World's Fastest Quarter Mile" and paved since 1974. Madison International (1/2 mile, 1952). La Crosse Fairgrounds (1957) [S75].
- The ASA (formerly ARCA) Midwest Tour, based in Oregon, WI, is the region's Super Late Model touring series [S75].
- Season is roughly April-September. The region has a strong SLM culture and produced Rich Bickle, Matt Kenseth, and others.

**Indiana**
- The center of USAC sprint and midget racing. Indiana Sprint Week draws about 40 cars a night, including at the IMS dirt track [S20].
- Home of the open-wheel shops, IndyCar, and Indy NXT. Many open-wheel drivers start in quarter midgets, move to USAC midgets and sprints, then go on to NASCAR or IndyCar.

**Pennsylvania (Central PA)**
- The "capital of sprint car racing in the world," centered on three weekly 410 tracks: Williams Grove, Port Royal, and Lincoln [S58].
- The PA Posse series runs 47 races at 11 tracks, paying $6,000 to win [S58]. Very high 410 density; many local "Posse" drivers race weekly against touring regulars.

**Ohio**
- Eldora Speedway hosts the Dream ($100,000), the World 100 ($72,000 → $100,000), and the Kings Royal ($200,000) [S66][S68].
- Dense mix of dirt late models and 410 sprints (approx.).

**Iowa / Nebraska**
- IMCA heartland. The IMCA Super Nationals in Boone, Iowa draw 1,100+ cars [S8].
- **Knoxville, IA** is the "Sprint Car Capital of the World." It races 410, 360, and Pro Sprint classes on Saturdays from April to September and hosts the Knoxville Nationals ($1M+ purse) [S67][S76].

**Missouri / Oklahoma / Kansas / Texas (Great Plains)**
- Lucas Oil Speedway in Wheatland, MO (3/8-mile dirt) hosts the Lucas Late Models, POWRi, USMTS, and ASCS [S77].
- The **Chili Bowl** runs in Tulsa every January with 300-385 midget entries [S19].
- USMTS dirt modifieds are "woven into the culture of the Great Plains states" [S78].
- Texas has a deep IMCA/USMTS modified and ASCS 360 sprint scene. Texas Motor Speedway's dirt track hosts High Limit [S78].

**North Carolina / Virginia**
- **Late Model Stock Car (LMSC)** country. The CARS LMSC Tour runs 15 races in NC, VA, SC, and GA [S32].
- The **Martinsville ValleyStar 300** draws about 77 entries for $32,000 to win [S61].
- Cup teams use LMSC programs for development (JR Motorsports, Lee Pulliam Performance) [S32].

**South Carolina / Georgia / Alabama / Tennessee**
- Super Late Model racing on asphalt (Southern SLM tours) and Dirt Late Model country, home to Lucas and WoO late model regulars (approx.).
- Pensacola, FL, at the Alabama line, hosts the **Snowball Derby** in December [S62].

**Florida (winter racing)**
- **World Series of Asphalt** at New Smyrna in February: 9 nights, 15 divisions, 215+ entries (2024) [S79].
- The Snowball Derby in December [S62].
- IMSA and NASCAR Speedweeks at Daytona [S27].
- Winter karting is strong (approx.).
- Florida is the main off-season venue for northern racers.

**New York / New England**
- Northeast modified culture. Big-block dirt modifieds race at Super DIRT Week in Oswego ($53,000 to win, October) [S64].
- Asphalt tour modifieds (NASCAR Whelen Modified Tour, Stafford) [S57].

**California / Southwest**
- Dirt sprint cars and midgets.
- The SRL Southwest Tour, a late-model feeder series since 2001 [S80].
- CARS Tour West SLMs at Kevin Harvick's Kern Raceway [S81].
- Irwindale closed in 2024 [S80].
- Strong road racing and karting presence (approx.).

**Pacific Northwest**
- Northwest Super Late Model Series: 8 races at 6 venues.
- Evergreen Speedway's Summer Showdown pays $30,000 to win and drew 30+ entries [S60].

**Arizona / Colorado**
- IMCA tracks in Arizona take part in the IMCA TV program [S59].
- Arizona has winter dirt racing (Arizona Speedway, approx.) and NASCAR at Phoenix.
- Colorado has a small mixed asphalt and dirt scene (approx., unverified).

**Seasonality**
- Northern tracks run **about April-September**; Knoxville, for example, races April-September [S76].
- The South runs longer seasons (about March-November).
- Winter racing is concentrated in Florida (New Smyrna in February, Pensacola in December), Tulsa (Chili Bowl in January), and Arizona/California.

**Travel costs**
- A national dirt late model tour uses about **$25,000 of diesel a season**, with a $250,000 hauler [S42].
- WoO spans 20+ states and Canada [S29]; High Limit runs 22 states [S30].
- Regional tours stay within about 300-500 miles (unverified).

### 3.3 International (short notes for future expansion)

- **Canada:**
  - The NASCAR Canada Series (Pinty's) runs Quebec- and Ontario-heavy fields, with CTMP as the season opener [S33].
  - The **APC United Late Model Series** is an Ontario asphalt LM tour [S82].
  - The **Maritimes** run Pro All Stars Series-style late models. Key event: the IWK 250 at Riverside International Speedway, Nova Scotia [S82].
- **Mexico:** the NASCAR Mexico Series completed its 18th season in 2025 (champion Alex de Alba) [S83]. Cup raced in Mexico City in 2025.
- **UK:**
  - The **750 Motor Club** is the largest club-racing provider, with about 2,000 members and 25+ championships [S84].
  - **BriSCA F1 stock cars** race on 1/4-mile shale or tarmac ovals with ~650 bhp V8s. The north is mostly BriSCA; Spedeworth owns 7 tracks in the south, and banger racing is common [S85].
  - **British F4** costs about £400,000 a season [S86].
  - **BTCC** costs about £300,000-750,000 per car [S86] (approx.).
- **Australia:**
  - **Supercars** runs 24 cars at about A$1.2-3M per car. **Super2** costs about A$800,000-1.2M [S87] (approx.).
  - Speedway sprintcars: the Grand Annual Classic (Warrnambool) pays A$60,000 to win in 2026 (A$40,000 in 2025), and the Kings Challenge pays A$10,000 [S88].
  - Bathurst is the Supercars crown jewel.
- **Europe:**
  - The ladder runs from karting to F4 (French F4 €174,600 with an arrive-and-drive package; Italian F4 €250,000+; typical €150,000-400,000) [S89], then FRECA/F3 (€1M+).
  - GT racing is a parallel pro path.
- **Brazil:** Stock Car Pro Series is in its 48th season (2026) and returns to V8s [S90].
- **Japan:** the ladder runs Super FJ → F4 Japan (Super GT support) → Super Formula / Super GT [S91].

## 4. Scouting and visibility

- **FloRacing** streams **1,000+ events a year**: dirt, pavement short tracks (every CARS Tour race), the Snowball Derby, and the World Series. It costs $179 a year or $39.99 a month [S92][S32].
- **DIRTVision**, owned by World Racing Group, covers WoO and DIRTcar [S93].
- **Racing America** covers APC, Northwest SLM, and other series. **IMCA TV** runs at 54 tracks [S59].
- **Aggregators:** DIRTRACKR's streaming list also includes RacinDirt, The Cushion, MAVTV Plus, SpeedSport.tv, and others [S94].
- **SCCA Super Tour streams:** 235,000+ views and 75,000+ hours watched in 2024 [S11].
- **TV tiers:**
  1. Cup, IndyCar, IMSA (network TV).
  2. O'Reilly, Trucks, ARCA (FS1/CW-level), plus Indy NXT and Road to Indy.
  3. Crown-jewel short-track and dirt events (pay-per-view streams).
  4. Weekly tracks (local streams, often none).
- **Exposure:** crown-jewel events (Snowball, Chili Bowl, Knoxville, Martinsville LMSC, the Dream, the World Series) put local racers in front of national scouts. Team owners and Cup drivers race them too: Larson races the Chili Bowl, and Dale Earnhardt Jr. races CARS events [S32][S65].
- **Region:** the NC/VA LMSC scene is visible to Charlotte teams, Indiana USAC to open-wheel teams and Toyota/Chevy development, and Iowa/Nebraska IMCA is mostly self-contained (low scouting).
