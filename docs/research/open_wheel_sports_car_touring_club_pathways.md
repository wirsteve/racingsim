# B. Career Pathways: American Open-Wheel, Sports Cars/Endurance, Touring Cars, Club Road Racing (2020s)

Research brief for an OOTP-style racing career management sim. Compiled Oct 2026 from web sources (listed at the end). Figures are USD unless noted. Anything marked **(approx.)** is an estimate or a range I inferred from indirect evidence. Anything marked **(unverified)** comes from general knowledge and I could not confirm it with a source this session.

## 1. Karting: the entry point

### 1.1 Age bands (US, SKUSA SuperNationals class rules)
| Class | Age | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Micro Swift (IAME) | 7–10 | first "national" class. Club "Kid Kart" starts at ~5 **(unverified)** |
| Mini Swift (IAME) | 9–13 | |
| KA100 Junior / X30 Junior | 12–15 | main junior national classes |
| KA100 Senior / X30 Senior | 15+ | |
| Pro Shifter / KZ-style | senior | top US class at SuperNats |

- **International (FIA Karting):** OK-Junior is for ages 12–14. OK (senior) is open from age 14 in the year of competition. The FIA added lower-cost OK-N / OKN-Junior and "Arrive & Drive" championships. In 2026 an Arrive & Drive entry cost about €3,950 per championship, down from €2,500 deposit + €5,450 per round. A World Championship OK/OKJ entry costs about €1,060–1,590, but that fee is a trivial share of a real season budget.
- **Major US series:** Superkarts! USA (SKUSA) runs the Pro Tour, Winter Series and SuperNationals in Las Vegas. SuperNationals has run since 1997 and drew more than 500 competitors in 2023. It calls itself the largest two-cycle karting event in the country, and both Michael Schumacher (2009) and Max Verstappen (2012) raced there. ROK Cup USA runs the Florida Winter Tour, which has four decades of history. In 2025 SKUSA and ROK Cup USA shared opening weekends at Orlando Kart Center. The US Pro Kart Series (USPKS) and US Open are other national series **(not independently verified this session)**.
- **Example elite karting arc:** Connor Zilisch started karting at age 5. He won the Mini ROK World title in Italy in 2017, beating 160+ entrants, and in 2020 became the first American to win the CIK-FIA Karting Academy Trophy. He moved to cars (SCCA Spec Miata) at 15. Kyle Kirkwood won SKUSA SuperNationals XVII in 2013 and received the Team USA Scholarship in 2016. Josef Newgarden started karting late, at 13, and won 4 titles in 2005–06.

### 1.2 Karting budgets
- Club or regional 100cc karting: about $5–10k per season to be competitive. First year all-in for KA100 / X100 / ROK VLR is about $11.5–20k.
- Regional and national championships: usually more than $30–40k per season.
- Top-tier international karting (factory or "works" teams in Europe): about $100k+ per year. Older sources cite about $100k per year for the former KF1 category.
- A UK article puts domestic karting at about £10k per year, with international karting "much higher".
- Whole-pathway estimate (godriveline.co): karting to F3 costs about €7.5M, and karting to F2 about €10.86M over ~14 years **(approx., single-source modelling)**.

## 2. American open-wheel: USF Pro Championships ("Road to Indy")

### 2.1 Structure (lowest to highest)
1. **USF Juniors** (founded 2022). Tatuus JR-23 with 2.0L Mazda MZR. The car costs about $94k complete and can be upgraded for USF2000 and USF Pro 2000. 6 events, 16–18 races. **Target budget $120–150k.**
2. **USF2000** (since 1990). Tatuus USF-17 / JR-23-based. Traditionally includes one oval: the 2025 calendar had 18 races (7 road courses, 2 street circuits, Indianapolis Raceway Park oval).
3. **USF Pro 2000** (since 1991, formerly Pro Mazda / Indy Pro 2000).
4. **Indy NXT** (current form since 2002, formerly Indy Lights). Dallara IL-15. The 2026 calendar has 17 races, including 3 ovals (Gateway, Nashville, Milwaukee).
5. **NTT IndyCar Series.**

USAC sanctions the lower rungs and IndyCar sanctions Indy NXT. Andersen Promotions runs USF Juniors, USF2000 and USF Pro 2000.

### 2.2 Minimum ages and licensing gates (official USF licensing guidelines, 2026)
| Series | Min. age (before first on-track session) | Experience required for automatic approval |
|---|---|---|
| USF Juniors | **14** | 6 amateur open-wheel races; OR 4 open-wheel + 10 kart races (or 6 race-school races); OR top-10 national karting ranking + 2 open-wheel races + 2 test days |
| USF2000 | **15** | 8 USF Juniors races with 6+ top-10s; OR 12 USF Juniors races; OR 12 races in a comparable open-wheel series |
| USF Pro 2000 | **16** | 8 USF2000 races with 6+ top-10s; OR 12 comparable races with competitive results |
| License fee | $700 per series per year | |

**Indy NXT to IndyCar licensing (new criteria for 2027, motorsport.com):**
- A driver needs at least **12 Indy NXT starts**.
- **"Competitively Qualified"** status comes from winning the NXT title, finishing top 3 in points, or winning 2+ races within 24 months.
- **"Evaluation Eligible"** status (allowed to do an IndyCar evaluation test) comes from 1 NXT win.
- Everyone else goes to **"Resume Review"**.
- Each status is valid for 24 months.
- Drivers from F1, F2, Formula E, Super Formula, IMSA GTP, NASCAR Cup or WEC Hypercar are approved directly or scheduled for evaluation.
- **Ovals:** a driver needs an approved oval test before an oval race unless IndyCar waives it.
- **Indy 500 Rookie Orientation Program (ROP):** this is mandatory for anyone who has never started the 500, and a refresher is required for anyone who has not raced an IndyCar oval since the previous 500.
  - ROP has 3 phases. Phase 1 is 10 laps at 205–210 mph, no faster.
  - A rookie must complete 2 of the 3 phases before practice and all 3 phases before a qualifying attempt.
  - A superspeedway ROP must come before the Indy ROP.

### 2.3 Scholarships and prize ladder (2025 season values)
| Achievement | Prize |
|---|---|
| USF Juniors champion | Scholarship of about **$249,675** to step up to USF2000. The 2026 package is $328k+, including a $251,516 scholarship |
| USF2000 champion | **$405,050** scholarship to USF Pro 2000. It was $458,400 for the 2024 season |
| USF Pro 2000 champion | **$546,500** advertised for 2025 (down $100k from the prior year). Max Garcia's cheque for the 2025 title was **$594,500**, for Indy NXT 2026 |
| Prize money across the three USF series | **$1.43M+** in prizes and awards |
| Indy NXT champion (2025) | **$850,000** scholarship, covering two IndyCar tests plus two race entries including the **Indianapolis 500**. Runner-up $125k, 3rd $65k |
| Indy NXT champion (Indy Lights era, historical) | $1M, with three guaranteed IndyCar races including the 500 |
| Whole ladder | A driver who wins all four rungs could collect **>$2M** in prizes |

**Other open-wheel scholarships and talent-ID events**
- **Team USA Scholarship.** Founded in 1990 by journalist Jeremy Shaw. It sends young Americans to the UK's Formula Ford Festival and Walter Hayes Trophy. Alumni include Newgarden (2008), Conor Daly, Oliver Askew, Kyle Kirkwood (2016) and Thomas Schrage (2022).
- **Mazda Road to Indy Shootout (2016–2018).** A knockout event for karting and junior open-wheel champions aged **14–25**, drawn from a network of official feeder series worldwide. The prize was a **$200k scholarship into USF2000**. Winners were Oliver Askew (2016) and Keith Donegan (2017).
- **Skip Barber Karting Scholarship Shootout (historical).** It ran annually; the 15th edition was in 2011 at Laguna Seca. Alumni include A.J. Allmendinger, Alexander Rossi, Sage Karam, Spencer Pigot and Conor De Phillippi. The championship shootout winner later received a $200k USF2000 scholarship.
- **Lucas Oil School of Racing Scholarship Shootout (around 2018–19).** Open to karters and later also to sim, quad and motocross racers. Winners chose $75k toward USF2000 or $50k toward F4. Skip Barber has since replaced Lucas Oil as the Road to Indy's official school.
- **Chris Griffis Memorial Test.** A two-day post-season test on the IMS road course, held every year. Teams use it to audition new drivers, and it works as a de-facto scouting combine.

**The funding cliff.** The USF Pro 2000 scholarship covers *less than half* an Indy NXT budget. Champions who could not find the money to move up include:
- **Simon Sikes**: won USF2000 by 100+ points but could not fund Indy NXT.
- **Alessandro de Tullio**: stepped away from cars for two years to regroup financially, then returned in 2025–26.
- **Michael d'Orlando, Kiko Porto and Braden Eves**: also cited as champions who stalled.

The same pattern shows up in Europe: Zak O'Sullivan quit F2 mid-2024 for lack of funds.

| Rung | Typical entry age | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| USF Juniors | 14–16 | 8 of the 2025 USF2000 rookies came up from Juniors |
| USF2000 | 15–21 | The 2025 grid ran from age 15 (Argyros, Musella, Garzon, Boshoff) to 21. 17–18 drivers, 8 teams |

**Years per rung:** stars spend **1 year per rung**. Kirkwood won USF2000 in 2018 (12 of 14 races), Indy Pro 2000 in 2019 and Indy Lights in 2021 (the 2020 season was cancelled). Hauger won Indy NXT as a rookie in 2025 (6 wins in 14 races) and moved to Dale Coyne IndyCar for 2026, where he was Rookie of the Year. Normal drivers spend **1–3 years per rung (approx.)** and often repeat a year to win the scholarship.

**Indy NXT as a pipeline:**
- 5 of the last 7 NXT champions are active in IndyCar in 2026.
- 6 of the 8 active NXT teams also run IndyCar programmes.
- The grid fell to 7 full-season cars in 2018, then grew to 20+ in 2024–25 and 24–28 in 2026.
- About one-third of the 2026 grid comes from outside the Americas, as European F2/F3 drivers "reassess the F1 ladder" because of cost. Examples are Victor Martins (2022 F3 champion), Enzo Fittipaldi (3 F2 seasons) and Juan Manuel Correa.

### 2.6 Parallel American open-wheel rungs (FIA-certified)
- **F4 United States.** The champion's scholarship to Formula Regional Americas was worth **$264,900** in 2025: a Ligier JS F3 chassis, a Mountune engine, Hankook tyres and entry fees.
- **Formula Regional Americas.** The champion gets **$100k cash**, 2nd $25k, 3rd $15k and the top rookie $5k.
- These series matter mainly because they award **FIA Super Licence points**. The USF series award few or none.

### 2.7 The European route and FIA Super Licence

- Age 18 or older at the first F1 event.
- An International Grade A licence.
- 80% of two full single-seater seasons.
- **40 points** over the best 3-year window (or 2 years plus the current year).
- **25 points** for an F1 free-practice licence.

## 3. IndyCar economics: pay drivers, salaries, Indy 500 one-offs

- **Charters.** Since September 2024 there are 25 charters, with a maximum of 3 per team, running through 2031. A charter guarantees a start at every race except the Indy 500, which stays open qualifying with 33 starters. Charter holders can access Leaders Circle money: Hauger's P22 finish secured the last Leaders Circle slot for Coyne, worth **$1.7M**. One charter sold for about $13M (Dreyer & Reinbold buying from RLL, as reported by RACER). Because the charters fill nearly the whole grid, talented drivers such as Enzo Fittipaldi and Caio Collet face a market where "talent, sponsorship and an available entry must align."
- **Who pays whom:**
  - Outside the top 3–4 teams, most drivers bring sponsorship. It can be a few hundred thousand dollars, or the entire ~$10M budget.
  - Top drivers earn **$1–3M** a year (some sources say up to $3–7M for stars).
  - Rookies and lesser-known drivers often earn little beyond a share of sponsorship and prize money.
  - Newgarden's rookie season (2012, at 21) came with a modestly funded team, Sarah Fisher Hartman.
- **Typical funding types:**
  1. Fully paid factory/team driver: Penske, Ganassi, McLaren, Andretti stars.
  2. Partially funded: the driver brings $1–5M from family or personal sponsors **(approx.)**.
  3. Full pay driver: the driver brings the whole budget.
  4. Scholarship rookie: the Indy NXT $850k covers tests, the 500 and one other race. A team still has to find the rest of the money for a full season.
- **Indy 500 one-offs.** Extra entries beyond the full-time grid are funded case by case. Associate stickers sell for about $30–50k. The cost of a one-off entry is **~$1–2.5M (approx./unverified)**. The 500 attracts veterans and crossover stars, and the ROP gates rookies.
- **Driver licensing:** see §2.2. The 2027 criteria make Indy NXT results the main road/street gate, with direct exemptions for top-tier international drivers.

### 4.2 MX-5 Cup: the clearest amateur-to-pro ladder
- **Minimum age** for IMSA membership in MX-5 Cup is **15**, and IMSA may lower it by one year for experienced drivers. The licence costs $975 per season, plus a physical and an ImPACT neurocognitive baseline test.
- **Feeder route:** a Mazda-powered champion at the SCCA Runoffs, NASA Championships or a regional Mazda challenge gets an automatic nomination to the **MX-5 Cup Shootout**. Nominees submit business plans and resumés and go through a two-day on- and off-track evaluation.
- **Shootout prizes:**
  - Earlier format: $260k total ($110k plus 2 × $75k). Zilisch won $110k in 2021.
  - 2025 format: $350k total. Max Stallone won $150k, and Ethan Lampe and Charlotte Traynor won $100k each, toward the 2026 season.
- **MX-5 Cup champion:** **$250k** scholarship. Jeremy Fletcher won it in 2025. Mazda pays out more than **$1M per year** in prize money and scholarships across the series.
- **Progression examples:**
  - **Connor Zilisch:** Spec Miata at 15 → Shootout winner in 2021 → MX-5 Cup in 2022 (4 wins, 2nd overall, Rookie of the Year) → won LMP2 at the Rolex 24 and Sebring 12h at 18 (2024) → NASCAR Xfinity (10 wins as a 2025 rookie).
  - **Nathanial Sparks:** 2016 champion and $200k "Mazda Road to 24" winner. He now owns Spark Performance, an MX-5 Cup prep shop, which shows the driver-to-team-owner exit path.
  - **Gresham Wagner:** MX-5 Cup champion in 2021 and again in 2024. Repeat champions show a driver can "stall" in a spec series.
- **IMSA 3D Scholarship:** IMSA's own driver development scholarship. Westin Workman was named the 2027–28 recipient. The amount and criteria could not be fetched **(unverified)**.

### 4.3 FIA Driver Categorisation: Platinum, Gold, Silver, Bronze (2025 regulations)
This system is the economic backbone of Pro-Am sports car racing.

- **No driver can be categorised before age 16.** Initial categorisation costs €150 (€350 for an emergency 7-day request). A review costs €250. Lists are published by 20 October and become final on 1 November.
- **Platinum:** a current or former Super Licence holder (practice licences count), or a top-5 finisher in a Tier 1 series. Also earned by:
  - a Pro-class podium at Le Mans, Daytona (GTP), Spa or the Nürburgring 24;
  - a WEC Hypercar or GTE Pro title;
  - an IMSA GTP title;
  - a GTWC Pro title.
- **Gold:** main professional activity is motorsport. Criteria include:
  - 3+ seasons of high-level karting;
  - 2+ significant single-seater seasons with a podium;
  - a career begun before age 20 with 5+ full seasons;
  - top-3 in a Tier 2 series or a win in a significant Tier 3 series;
  - good results in LMP2, LMP3, LMGT3 or GT3 Am-type classes.
- **Silver:**
  - any driver under 30, even in their first licensed year;
  - any driver earning income from motorsport;
  - a driver over 30 who started racing in their 20s or earlier.
  - **Drivers under 27 stay at least Silver.** A Silver is not automatically downgraded to Bronze at 30; they must apply with data.
- **Bronze:** a driver whose **first licence came after age 30**, rated on a performance basis.
- **Data-driven moves.**
  - Upgrade: a driver who is as fast as or faster than the average of the higher category in 30% or more of races in a season.
  - Downgrade: a driver who is as slow as or slower than the higher category's average in 30% or more of races over **3+ consecutive seasons**. Only allowed from age 27, and only seasons with at least 75% race participation count.
  - Pace is measured on the 10 best race laps (or 20 laps in races over 2 hours).
- **Age downgrades:**
  - −1 grade the season after the **55th** birthday;
  - another −1 after the **60th**;
  - Bronze after the **65th**;
  - no downgrade at the end of a year in which the driver won a category title.
  - Drivers aged 30–40 who have been out of racing for 10+ years, or 40+ out for 5+ years, may return one grade lower.
  - Injury or illness can also allow a one-grade drop, which is provisional.
- **Tiers used for categorisation:**
  - **Tier 1:** F2/GP2, any FIA World Championship, IndyCar, Super Formula, V8 Supercars, WTCC/WTCR, Formula E.
  - **Tier 2:** F3/GP3, Porsche Supercup, NASCAR Cup, DTM, Super GT, FIA World Cups.
  - **Tier 3:** national touring car series, Carrera Cups, F4, LMP3, national GT, NASCAR Xfinity/Trucks, international karting.

**How the ratings create demand for paying amateurs:**
- **IMSA GTD:** at least one Silver or Bronze driver per car and no more than one Platinum. In endurance races, at most one Platinum or Gold. GTD has had a Bronze-driver mandate since 2023: in 2022, only 1 of 11 Road America GTD entries already met it. Daytona requires 4 drivers.
- **IMSA LMP2:** at least one Bronze in endurance races and at most one Gold.
- **WEC LMGT3:** each crew of 2–3 needs at least one Bronze plus another Bronze or a Silver. The Bronze must do qualifying 1 and the Silver does Hyperpole. Silver/Bronze minimum drive time is 1h45 per 6h race, 2h20 per 8h and 2h55 per 10h.
- **Result:** every Pro-Am car needs a rated amateur, who usually brings most or all of the budget. Fast Bronzes are scarce. Teams compete in a "race to the bottom" on price to land the best gentlemen, and an over-performing Bronze risks being upgraded out of value.

### 4.4 How pros get paid
Figures from Marshall Pruett's RACER salary survey (2022), as summarised by search results:

| Category | Pro salary range |
|---|---|
| GTD | $80k–225k, average about **$150–160k**. Few earn above $250k. Brand-ambassador types reach $325–350k |
| LMP2 | Most pros earn **$200–300k** |
| DPi/GTP | $300–400k. Star names reach about $500k |

- Works GT drivers in IMSA, WEC and DTM earn about **£150k–£1M** a season, depending on category and results (TenTenths forum, approx.).
- Most working pros cluster at about **$20k–200k**, made up from race fees, coaching and instructing.
- **Coach-driver role.** Gentleman drivers hire pros as driving coaches and as Pro-Am co-drivers. Coaching day rates are about $1–3k per day **(unverified)**. Many mid-career pros make most of their income from coaching Bronzes and from Pro-Am seats that the Bronze pays for.

### 4.5 Manufacturer and factory programmes
- **Porsche:**
  - North American Junior Program (Carrera Cup NA since 2021, Sprint Challenge since 2025). It includes training (tyres, chassis, media, fitness) plus incentives such as free entries and tyres.
  - Porsche runs a scouting "pyramid": karting → Carrera Cup → GT → GTP.
  - The North American Junior winner goes to the **global Porsche Junior Shootout**. Each of the 12 Carrera Cups nominates one driver aged **up to 23** (in practice 18–23). The shootout runs 4 days in Cologne and at Estoril. The winner gets up to **€225k** for a Porsche Supercup season.
  - Path: Porsche Junior → Porsche "Selected Driver" → factory driver. A Female Driver programme also exists.
- **Honda/Acura (HPD):** a three-step ladder of GT3 Driver Academy (run by Racers Edge, with the NSX GT3 and Civic Type R TCR) → Junior Factory Driver → Factory Driver. There is also an HPD Super Formula scholarship to Japan. HRC is **pausing the Acura factory GTP programme after 2026** to expand IndyCar, which shows factory seats can disappear.
- **Toyota:**
  - The GR Cup is described as a "huge propellant" for driver development: GR Cup → TCR or GT4 (Pirelli GT4 America).
  - Toyota Driver Development (TD2) covers stock cars and sports cars.
  - A GR Customer Racing **GT Young Driver Academy** (2026) leads toward the Lexus/Toyota GT3 roster.
  - TGR-DC is Toyota's global programme.
- **Mazda:** the Spec Miata → MX-5 Cup ladder described above. Mazda no longer runs a factory prototype programme, so its ladder now hands graduates to other brands **(unverified that no factory step exists in 2026)**.
- **Others (unverified this session):**
  - BMW M junior/factory programme;
  - Chevrolet/Corvette factory pros and Pratt Miller;
  - Ford Performance (Mustang GT3/GT4);
  - Aston Martin "Young Driver AMR";
  - Ferrari Driver Academy (F1-focused) and Ferrari Competizioni GT driver academy;
  - Lamborghini GT3 Junior Programme;
  - Lexus GT3 via Vasser Sullivan.

## 5. Touring cars

### 5.1 North America
- **TC America** (SRO). Classes are TCR, TCX and TC. It is described as "the most cost effective championship for professional touring car racing in North America". A Honda Civic Type R TC car costs **$89,900**. The champion's "Level Up Award" pays a full season of entry fees in **Pirelli GT4 America**.
- **IMSA Michelin Pilot Challenge, TCR class.** 2–3 driver endurance races of about 2–4 hours. Pro-Am lineups are common. Seats are sold per event: a 2019 Daytona TCR one-off was about $18–27.5k per seat.

### 5.2 UK: BTCC
- Ladder: **Ginetta Junior** (age 14–17; minimum 14 before the first race) → British F4 (from 15), Ginetta GT Supercup/GT4, or TCR UK → **BTCC**.
- The **Jack Sears Trophy** goes to the top rookie (since 2015).
- Budgets: about **£250k per season** for a top independent-team seat, or about £50k for a self-run car (forum estimates, approx./dated). The car (minus engine) is about £100k, and the engine about £25k or leased.

### 5.3 Australia: Supercars ladder
- **Super3 → Super2 → Supercars.**
  - Super3 was merged into Super2 and dropped as a separate class by 2025 because grids were small.
  - Super2 runs 6 rounds on the Supercars bill.
  - Older Car-of-the-Future cars (A$100–150k) became eligible again in 2025 to cut costs.
- **Super2 budget:** up to A$600k for a top car (2023). Other estimates are A$600–750k, or A$0.8–1.2M for a competitive season. A ~$500k Super2 prize pot was withdrawn, and the funding programme was postponed.
- **Motorsport Australia Superlicence** (since 2017), required for Supercars:
  - age **17+** and an FIA International Grade C licence;
  - **15 points over 5 seasons**. Super2 positions 1–6 earn 15 points; Super3 or Carrera Cup top-4 earn 12; F4 or Trans-Am wins earn 7; karting earns up to 3;
  - alternatively, 6 Super2 rounds, or 3 rounds after a top-3 finish in Super3 or Carrera Cup.
- "The vast majority" of Supercars drivers have raced at least one Super2 season. Graduates include McLaughlin (2012 champion), Cam Waters (2015), Bryce Fullwood (2019), Broc Feeney (2021) and Rylan Gray (2025 champion, Tickford).

### 5.4 Case studies
- **Shane van Gisbergen (NZ):** ATVs and quarter midgets as a child → brief karting → Formula First via a magazine scholarship → Formula Vee (2005 rookie of the year) → NZ Formula Ford champion (2006) → Toyota Racing Series runner-up (2007) → **Supercars debut at 18** (2007, Stone Brothers). He won the Supercars title 3 times (2016, 2021, 2022) and then moved to NASCAR. The pattern is cheap single-seaters at home, then early touring car signing with no European detour.
- **Scott McLaughlin (NZ/AUS):**
  - Karting from 1999, with his first title in 2002; his family moved to the Gold Coast in 2003.
  - Development Series (Super2) debut in 2010 at 16, then the youngest person to race a V8 Supercar.
  - Super2 champion in 2012; Supercars co-drive in 2012 and full-time with GRM from 2013 at 19–20; youngest race winner.
  - Supercars champion three times (2018–20), then joined **Team Penske in IndyCar at 27** (debut Oct 2020).
  - Raced IMSA LMP2 from 2023.
  - Shows that a touring car star can cross over into IndyCar.

## 6. Grassroots and club road racing

### 6.1 SCCA (Sports Car Club of America)
- **Size:** 65,000+ annual members across about 115 regions. National dues are $85 individual or $120 family, plus $10–20 regional dues.
- **Licensing path:**
  1. Join SCCA. Complete the online drivers-school modules. Upload a state driver's licence.
  2. Medical: minors need a doctor's form valid 5 years; adults sign an annual declaration; ages 60–69 need a doctor's form every 2 years, and 70+ every year.
  3. Buy a **Novice Permit**. It is valid for **2 years** and is available from **age 14** with parental consent. A minor without a road driver's licence needs prior racing experience and an interview with the Divisional Licensing Administrator.
  4. Attend an SCCA drivers school or Race Experience event for sign-off.
  5. Complete **3 successful race weekends** to earn the **Full Competition License**. This license unlocks Regional, Majors, Hoosier Super Tour and Runoffs events.
  6. **Shortcut:** an accredited "Level 4" race school allows the driver to buy the full licence directly.
  7. Minors are those under 18 in most states (under 19 in AL/NE, under 21 in MS).
- **Event tiers:** Regional → Conference **Majors** → **Hoosier Super Tour** (national) → **Runoffs**.
- **Runoffs qualification (U.S. Majors Tour path):**
  - Start **3+ Majors or Super Tour events** and finish **3+ races**. A finish means completing 50% or more of the winner's laps.
  - One Majors weekend can be swapped for 2 Regional weekends.
  - The driver must place in the **top 50%** of the class points standings in a Conference or the Super Tour, or top 10 when a class has fewer than 20 participants.
- **The Runoffs ("Olympics of amateur road racing"):**
  - First held in 1964 as the ARRC, named "Runoffs" in 1987.
  - The 2025 edition at Road America had **23 classes** (consolidated from more) and about **408 competitors**.
  - The record is **969 entries** (2017, Indianapolis).
  - 105 drivers were Runoffs first-timers in 2024.
  - Long careers are common: Mark Weber has 69 starts and was racing at age 70; Richard Colburn has 44 consecutive years. John Heinricy holds the record with 16 wins.
  - **Youngest champion:** Ethan Goulart, Spec Miata 2024, at **15 years 174 days**, in a 45-car field. His father, a multi-time champion, finished 4th.
- **Key classes:**
  - **Spec Miata.** The most popular road racing class in SCCA and NASA. Fields are often 20+ at regionals, 50+ at big events and 45 at the 2024 Runoffs. NASA capped its Nationals entry at 65 in 2008.
    - Car: $6–10k (regional, midpack), $15–35k (pro-built), up to $65k (proven winners).
    - About $1k per weekend all-in on average. One regional champion ran his whole 2023 season for under $7k. A front-running national programme is about $50k per season. First-year all-in is $30–70k.
  - **Spec MX-5:** the newest national class, on the Runoffs since 2024.
  - **Formula Vee:** the cheapest open-wheeler. Cars cost from $4k; a rented arrive-and-drive is about $2k per race; entries are $300–700.
  - **Formula Enterprises 2 (FE2):** Mazda MZR, about 185 hp, 6-speed sequential. Used cars are $15–35k, so a driver can start for well under $50k.
  - **Spec Racer Ford Gen3:** a Ford 1.6L, 135 hp spec racer.
  - **F1000, Formula Atlantic and others:** more expensive amateur open-wheel classes **(unverified details)**.

### 6.2 NASA (National Auto Sport Association, founded 1991)
- **HPDE ladder:** HPDE 1 (with an instructor) → 2 → 3 → 4. There are no speed limits, but passing rules relax as drivers move up.
- **Competition license:**
  - Requirements: about **8+ days in HPDE4** (or a strong recommendation from the HPDE4 group leader), NASA membership, a physical and an open-book rules test.
  - **Competition School:** a few times a year per region. Drivers can use their HPDE street car.
  - Graduates get a **Provisional License**, which becomes a full licence after **4 clean races**.
- NASA has "thousands" of members; the exact count was not found **(unverified)**. Its season finale is the NASA Championships.

### 6.3 Club racing's role in the career system
- **Late starters and adult entry:** HPDE → club racing. Bronze categorisation keys on a first licence after 30, so club racing is exactly where future paying Bronzes are made.
- **Juniors:** Zilisch and Goulart show 14–15-year-olds using Spec Miata as a stepping stone from karting into MX-5 Cup.
- **Destination for retired pros and lifers:** multi-decade Runoffs careers are common. Ex-pros race SCCA, vintage and Pro-Am events and coach **(qualitative)**.
- **Formal links to the pro ladder:**
  - Mazda Runoffs or NASA championship → MX-5 Cup Shootout.
  - Historically, the Runoffs fed the Mazda Road to Indy USF2000 Shootout.
  - SCCA Pro Racing (a separate entity) sanctions F4 US and FR Americas.

## 7. How teams and scouts discover drivers
1. **Results in scholarship series.** Scholarships ride on championships (USF, MX-5 Cup, F4 US to FRA), so a title is the best "scouting report."
2. **Shootouts.**
   - Mazda Road to Indy ($200k, 2016–18) and Skip Barber and Lucas Oil (historical).
   - MX-5 Cup Shootout ($260–350k total), Porsche Junior Shootout (€225k), Team USA Scholarship.
3. **Post-season tests:** the Chris Griffis Memorial Test (IMS road course) and IndyCar evaluation tests under the 2027 licensing criteria.
4. **Karting team owners, driver coaches and management.**
   - Development outfits such as Jay Howard Driver Development place drivers in the USF series.
   - Brad Majman was signed by Fernando Alonso's management group.
   - Coaches double as talent spotters **(qualitative)**.
5. **Sim racing.**
   - **Jann Mardenborough:** won Nissan/PlayStation **GT Academy** in 2011 at 19, beating ~90,000 entrants. He reached an LMP2 podium at Le Mans in 2014 and raced in GP3 and Super GT.
   - **Rudy van Buren:** an ex-karter and sales manager who won McLaren's **World's Fastest Gamer** in 2017 (30,000+ entrants) and became McLaren's F1 simulator driver.
   - **James Baldwin:** won WFG season 2 (a prize worth ~$1M) → GT3 with Jenson Team Rocket RJN. He won the Silver class on his British GT debut in 2020 and joined the BRDC Rising Stars.
   - Lucas Oil also opened its shootout to sim racers.
   - Sim is a rare, high-variance route that skips the funding gate, and it usually leads into GT/sports cars rather than open-wheel.
6. **Manufacturer pyramids:** Porsche's scouting from karting upward and the Toyota and HPD academies.

## 8. Money: family wealth, sponsorship, management, the funding cliff
- Up to Indy NXT or F3, almost all drivers are **pay-to-play**: family money, family business sponsorship or personal sponsors.
- Scholarships pay for one rung but not the next, and costs roughly double at each rung:

| Rung | Approx. season cost |
|---|---|
| USF Juniors | $0.15M |
| USF2000 | ~$0.45M |
| USF Pro 2000 | ~$0.6M |
| Indy NXT | $1.2–1.5M |
| IndyCar seat (sponsorship to bring) | $1–10M |

- In sports cars, amateurs fund the cars and the pros' salaries. A talented but unfunded young driver can become a salaried pro by being the "pro" half of a Pro-Am lineup, by coaching, or through a manufacturer junior programme. This makes sports cars the main **soft landing** for open-wheel drivers who hit the funding cliff.
- In sports cars, a pro "drops out" when they are rated up to Gold/Platinum without factory backing: they become too expensive for a Pro-Am lineup but are not good enough for a factory seat **(qualitative inference)**.
- Agents and management: driver-management firms (for example Alonso's A14 Management) and karting team owners broker seats. The pay model is commission or equity in future earnings **(unverified specifics)**.

## 9. Geography of US road racing
- **Southeast:** Daytona and Sebring (FL), Road Atlanta (GA; Petit Le Mans), VIR (VA), Barber (AL) and Charlotte Roval (NC). Winter karting and testing cluster in Florida (Orlando Kart Center and the Florida Winter Tour).
- **Midwest:** Road America (WI; June Sprints with 570+ racers in 2024; Runoffs 2024–26), Mid-Ohio (OH), Indianapolis (IN; IMS road course, Indy 500, Chris Griffis test, IRP oval), Gateway (IL), Milwaukee Mile (WI) and Detroit (MI).
- **West:** Laguna Seca, Long Beach and Sonoma (CA), Thunderhill and Buttonwillow (CA club tracks), Portland (OR) and Las Vegas (SKUSA SuperNationals).
- **Northeast:** Watkins Glen (NY), Lime Rock (CT) and the New Jersey and New England club tracks. Canadian Tire Motorsport Park (Mosport, ON) also hosts IMSA.
- **Texas/South:** COTA (Austin) and NOLA.
- The IMSA 2025 calendar shows the national weighting: endurance races at Daytona, Sebring, Watkins Glen, Road Atlanta and Indianapolis; sprints at Long Beach, Laguna Seca, Detroit, Mosport, Road America and VIR.
- **International pull:**
  - Ambitious open-wheel juniors go to Europe at 14–16 (OK karting, F4, FRECA) for Super Licence points.
  - A reverse flow brings Europeans into Indy NXT, about one-third of the 2026 grid.
  - Sports car pros aim for WEC and Le Mans.
  - Australians and New Zealanders go to Supercars and, increasingly, to IndyCar/NASCAR (McLaughlin, van Gisbergen).
  - The Team USA Scholarship sends US juniors to UK Formula Ford.

\*Funding/performance weight is a suggested mix: the share of the "can the driver get and keep this seat" score that comes from money versus results/skill.

### 10.2 Advancement probabilities (suggested starting points; tune in playtesting) (approx.)
- **Champion of a USF rung advances next year:** about 85–90%. The scholarship covers the first year, but a few champions still stall on the remaining budget gap. Use about 70% for Indy NXT and above.
- **Non-champion top-5 advances:** about 50%, scaled strongly by funding.
- **Midfield or backmarker advances:** about 15–25%, almost entirely funding-driven. Repeat-year probability is about 30–40%.
- **Indy NXT champion gets a full-time IndyCar seat within 1 year:** about 70%. 5 of the last 7 champions are active, and the charter system tightens the market.
- **Indy NXT non-champion reaches IndyCar full time:** about 15–25%. A driver with $3M+ in sponsorship should get a big bonus.
- **Open-wheel driver who stalls at USF Pro 2000 or Indy NXT pivots to sports cars:** about 40%. The rest pivot to coaching or quit, which is the funding cliff.
- **MX-5 Cup Shootout winner becomes a paid sports car pro within 4 years:** about 30–40% **(approx.)**.
- **Porsche Junior winner becomes a factory driver within 3–4 years:** about 50–60% **(approx.)**.
- **Club racer (Spec Miata) reaches a paid pro career:** under 2% overall. For a nominated Mazda champion aged 15–22, use about 10–20% **(approx.)**.

### 10.3 Licensing and eligibility gates to model
1. **Age gates:**
   - kart classes by age band;
   - USF Juniors 14, USF2000 15, USF Pro 2000 16;
   - MX-5 Cup 15 (14 with an experience waiver);
   - SCCA Novice Permit 14 (with experience if no road licence);
   - FIA categorisation from 16;
   - Motorsport Australia Superlicence 17;
   - FIA Super Licence 18;
   - Ginetta Junior 14, British F4 15.
2. **Experience gates:** USF race-count rules (§2.2); SCCA 3 race weekends within 2 years; NASA HPDE4 (8+ days) → Comp School → 4 clean races.
3. **IndyCar 2027 licence tiers:** Competitively Qualified / Evaluation Eligible / Resume Review, based on Indy NXT results with a 12-start minimum. Add a separate oval test flag and an **Indy 500 ROP** flag (3 phases; Phase 1 at 205–210 mph; superspeedway ROP first).
4. **FIA Super Licence:** 40 points over 3 years using the table in §2.7, an 80% two-season single-seater requirement and age 18+.
5. **FIA Driver Categorisation** as a dynamic driver attribute:
   - initial rating from age and career record;
   - performance-based moves (the 30%-of-races rule; downgrade only from age 27, after 3 seasons);
   - age downgrades at 55, 60 and 65;
   - "Bronze" only for drivers first licensed after 30.
   - Series lineup rules then constrain team lineups: GTD needs a Silver or Bronze and at most one Platinum; LMP2 needs a Bronze; LMGT3 needs a Bronze plus a Silver or Bronze.
6. **Runoffs qualification:** 3 Majors/Super Tour events, 3 finishes, top 50% in points.
7. **Supercars Superlicence:** 15 points over 5 seasons, or the Super2 round-count alternatives.

### 10.4 Economic mechanics worth simulating
- **Scholarship as a partial voucher.** The value is tied to the next rung but covers about 40–100% of it. Example: the USF Pro 2000 scholarship ($0.55–0.6M) against an Indy NXT season ($1.2–1.5M) is about 40%. Model a "budget gap" that the player, family or sponsors must close.
- **Cost inflation per rung:** about ×2 per step in US open-wheel and about ×1.5–2 per step in European single-seaters.
- **Pro-Am market:**
  - Each Pro-Am car needs one rated amateur who pays $1–2M per season (GTD).
  - Pros earn $80–300k; factory pros earn $150k–1M+.
  - Bronze drivers are a scarce, demand-driven resource, so teams should compete for them, with price discounting.
  - An over-performing Bronze may be upgraded to Silver, which lowers their market value to teams.
- **Charter scarcity in IndyCar:** 25 charters at about $13M each. Non-charter entries are risky. The Indy 500 is open, which allows one-off entries for funded drivers.
- **Income streams for mid-tier pros:** salary + coaching (unverified ~$1–3k per day) + prize money + personal sponsorship. Coaching is a viable "career mode" for drivers who stall.
- **Late-career arcs:**
  - Gentleman entry: first licence at 30–50, enters as Bronze, and can race into the 70s (Runoffs examples).
  - Pros drift downward through age-based categorisation drops at 55, 60 and 65, which makes them attractive Pro-Am picks again.
- **Alternative talent pipelines:** low-probability, high-payoff events (sim-racing competitions, scholarship shootouts, a karting world title) that bypass funding. Example: GT Academy's 1-in-90,000 winner was in a Le Mans podium within 3 years.
