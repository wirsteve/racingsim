# Team & Driver Economics, 1995-2026 (sourced ranges)

Research for the racingsim economy model. Compiled 2026-10-06 by web research.

**How to read this file**
- All money is **nominal US dollars for the year stated** (not inflation-adjusted).
- Every figure gives what it measures, the year it applies to, a source URL, and a confidence level.
- Confidence: **high** means a primary document, sworn testimony, or an official release. **Medium** means a reputable outlet citing named people or documents. **Low** means an aggregator or agency blog, a single anecdote, or a figure seen only in a search-result summary.
- "(search summary only)" marks a page I could not open, because it was blocked (HTTP 403/429) or paywalled. The figure comes from the search engine's summary of that page and is **unverified**. No blocks were bypassed.
- **EST** marks an estimate I derived myself. The arithmetic is shown.
- Related repo research (local purses, field sizes, scholarships) is in `docs/research/pyramid_economics_geography.md` and `docs/research/knowledge_economics.md`. This file does not repeat them except where needed.

## 1. Team operating budgets

### 1.1 NASCAR Cup Series

**Current era (2022-2026, Next Gen car, charters)**
- **Cost to field one car for all 38 races is ~$20M.** This is Denny Hamlin's and Bob Jenkins' (Front Row) testimony at the antitrust trial in Dec 2025. Confidence: high. https://www.nbcsports.com/nascar/news/denny-hamlin-testifies-that-signing-nascars-charter-deal-would-have-been-a-death-certificate ; https://www.nbcsports.com/nascar/news/nascar-president-testifies-france-family-was-opposed-to-new-revenue-model
- **2024 team financials** come from trial exhibits, as reported by Fox Sports on 2025-11-11, covering 12 organizations with teams unnamed. Confidence: high. https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nascar/nascar-financials-revealed-profits-losses
  - Revenue per car (charter + purse + sponsorship) ranged from **$8.2M** at the lowest-earning organization to **$43M** at the top team.
  - The weighted average was a **loss of $2.203M per car**. Only **3 of 12** organizations made money, and only one made more than $150k per car. One team said it lost **$10M per car**.
- **23XI** was profitable in 4 of 5 seasons (by more than $2M in 2022 and more than $3.5M in 2023). Front Row lost about $60M over the last decade and about $100M since 2004 (Jenkins' testimony). Confidence: high. https://www.nbcsports.com/nascar/news/denny-hamlin-testifies-that-signing-nascars-charter-deal-would-have-been-a-death-certificate ; https://abcnews.go.com/Sports/wireStory/2-teams-suing-nascar-court-compete-new-charter-114641775
- **Next Gen car cost.** The car was intended to cost ~$205k but in practice cost ~$150-200k more, so roughly $350-400k each (Jenkins' testimony). Confidence: high. https://www.nbcsports.com/nascar/news/nascar-president-testifies-france-family-was-opposed-to-new-revenue-model
- **Car-cost line per car** (lawsuit filings, search summary only):
  - 23XI: ~$2.8M in 2021 (Gen-6), then $3.1-4.3M per year in 2022-24.
  - Front Row: ~$2.9M in 2021, rising to ~$7.1M in 2024.
  - Confidence: low-medium. https://www.essentiallysports.com/nascar-news-true-burden-of-nascars-next-gen-surfaces-as-michael-jordans-23xi-frm-suffer-millions-in-car-costs/
- **Typical line items** (explainer article, not primary):
  - $15-25M per car per season.
  - Engine lease $0.8-1.5M per year.
  - Simulator investment $1-5M.
  - Sponsorship is 60-70% of team income.
  - Confidence: low-medium. https://speedwaydigest.com/index.php/news/regional-racing-news/890911-how-nascar-teams-make-money-and-why-charters-hold-so-much-value-today/
- **Single-car vs multi-car.** No direct per-car comparison has been published (Fox says the exhibits do not split it). Indirect evidence:
  - In 2008 the 6 most valuable teams ran 22 of 54 cars and took 80% of top-20 points positions. One- and two-car teams were described as "increasingly uncompetitive" (Forbes, via NBC News 2008-07-24). Confidence: medium. https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna25834726
  - NASCAR caps ownership at 4 charters per organization. https://speedwaydigest.com/index.php/news/regional-racing-news/890911-how-nascar-teams-make-money-and-why-charters-hold-so-much-value-today/
  - Small teams buy technical alliances instead. Furniture Row's JGR alliance cost rose ~300% before the team closed (see section 9).

  - Budweiser's spend on Earnhardt Jr. was estimated at ~$15M a year, and a "generic" annual sponsorship cost was cited as $20M per car.
  - Roush sponsorship revenue was $100M, against race winnings of ~$8M.
  - RCR revenue was $56M.
  - Source: NBC News/Forbes 2006-05-18. Confidence: medium. https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna12861186
- **2007:** The average team value was ~$120M, with average team revenue of $84M (Forbes, search summary only). Confidence: low. https://www.jayski.com/2007/06/22/the-most-valuable-nascar-teams/
- **2008:** Forbes team values were Hendrick $335M, Roush Fenway $313M, JGR $184M and Petty $44M. The average was $119M. Confidence: medium. https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna25834726
- **2014:** About $15M per season (≈$400k per week over 38 weeks). The same piece separately quotes SBJ's 2014 Stewart-Haas data of "over $1.4M per race", which is probably an organization-level figure, not per car. Car ~$200k, engine ~$100k. Source: NBC (2017-05-25) citing SportsBusiness Journal 2014. Confidence: medium. https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/national-international/race-car-costs-the-high-price-of-fielding-a-racing-team/2016238/

### 1.2 Xfinity / Busch / Nationwide / O'Reilly Series
- **2025 detailed budget** (former Xfinity crew chief, The Autopian, 2025-11-13). Confidence: medium. https://www.theautopian.com/an-extraordinarily-detailed-accounting-of-how-much-it-really-costs-to-race-in-nascar
  - **$4.0M per year is the "bare minimum" for a sustainable mid-pack car** (~$121k per race over 33 races).
  - Fixed costs total $2.53M: engine lease $800k, tires $470k (188 sets × $2,500), payroll $674.5k for 8 people, pit crew rental $260k, entry fees $73.6k.
  - Semi-fixed costs $312k. Variable costs $512k, including crash and body repair of $464k.
  - Purse income: about $1.65M is paid out per standard race across the field, which averages ~$46k per team per race.
  - The team must raise ≥$2.5M per year from sponsors or the driver.
  - Startup costs are $0.8-1.0M (hauler $300-500k, 7 chassis at $35-50k each, pull-down rig). The shop is extra: rent of $122.5k per year, or a ~$3.4M purchase.
- **"Quality" Xfinity ride: ~$6M a season** (Hailie Deegan, Fox Sports, 2025-01-28). Confidence: medium. https://www.foxsports.com/articles/xfinity/hailie-deegan-puts-nascar-in-rearview-mirror-as-she-fails-to-find-funding-for-top-xfinity-seat
- **2008:** Phoenix Racing's independent Nationwide team had a ~$6M budget, and a typical full season cost $3.5-5M. Frontstretch 2008-11-06; search summary only (403). Confidence: low. https://frontstretch.com/2008/11/06/phoenix-racing-on-the-rise-again
- **TV money share:** As of 2014, Xfinity teams received 5.75% of the teams' TV pool (Cup 93.75%, Trucks 0.5%). Source: Tommy Joe Martins, 2017. Confidence: medium. https://jeffgluck.com/tommy-joe-martins-nascar-tv-revenue-model-small-teams/

### 1.3 Truck Series
- **2017:** Kyle Busch said it cost **$3.2M** to run a full truck team and should be ~$2M (Fox Sports, 2017-05-25). Confidence: high (owner quote). https://www.foxsports.com/stories/nascar/kyle-busch-talks-about-real-cost-of-going-racing
- **2017:** Brad Keselowski said it cost ~**$4.5M per truck per season**, and that he lost ~**$1M per year**. He shut BKR down after 2017. Confidence: high. https://jeffgluck.com/brad-keselowski-racing-close-after-2017
- **2015 small team:** Martins Motorsports earned ~$14k per race in prize money. TV share was about $1,000 per race per team. Entry fee was $1,650, and a tire set cost ~$2,300, with 5 sets per weekend. Confidence: medium. https://jeffgluck.com/tommy-joe-martins-nascar-tv-revenue-model-small-teams/

### 1.5 Asphalt short-track late models (LMSC, Pro/Super Late Model, regional tours)
- **Late Model Stock:**
  - In 1994-97 a full year cost ~**$50-60k**, with an engine at $10-15k.
  - The same program cost **$150-200k** a season in 2018 (Dale Earnhardt Jr., Short Track Scene, 2018-12-16). Confidence: high.
  - EST: a ~3-3.5× nominal rise (175/55 ≈ 3.2).
  - https://www.shorttrackscene.com/late-model-stock-cars/dale-earnhardt-jr-opines-on-late-model-stock-engine-debate-tire-prices/
- **Engines (2022):** A Super Late Model engine is $25-30k (~630 hp). A Late Model Stock crate-spec engine is ~$10k in parts (~510 hp). Confidence: medium. https://speedsport.com/nascar/other-stock-cars/touring-late-models/late-models-super-vs-stock/
- **Season budgets:**
  - Super Late Model regional tour (CARS Tour, ASA STARS, ARCA/ASA Midwest Tour): about $100-250k. The CARS LMSC tour is about $150-300k. Both are unverified approximations from the repo's earlier research. Confidence: low. See `pyramid_economics_geography.md` table rows 100-101.
- **1996 local sponsorship:** The largest sponsorships at a local track (Langley Speedway, VA) were up to ~$30k, against $2-10M in Winston Cup. Confidence: medium. https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/VA-Pilot/issues/1996/vp960711/07110556.htm
- **Snowball Derby:** Teams hired NASCAR pit crews for ~$5k a race when the race paid $25k to win. The cost led the race to eliminate live pit stops in 2019. Confidence: medium. https://www.shorttrackscene.com/super-late-models/snowball-derby/snowball-derby-eliminates-live-pit-stops-for-2019-race/

## 2. NASCAR Cup charter system

### 2.1 Structure (2016-2024)
- Announced **2016-02-09**:
  - **36 charters**, each guaranteeing entry to every points race.
  - Fields cut from 43 to **40**, leaving **4 open spots**.
  - **Nine-year** term.
  - A charter can be removed if its team finishes in the bottom 3 of the 36 charter teams in owner points **3 years running**.
  - A charter can be leased for one full season once in the first five years.
  - Charters were awarded to teams that ran full-time 2013-15.
  - Confidence: high. https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2016/02/09/nascar-implements-team-owner-charter-agreement-for-sprint-cup-series/amp/ ; https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2017/01/10/nascar-charter-system-fast-facts-ownership-setup/amp/
- Before charters, drivers had individual contracts with "specific salaries and percentages of each week's purse". The charter system replaced the published purse with fixed and performance pools paid to charter teams, and purses stopped being published (Fox News/AP, 2016-04-28). Confidence: medium. https://www.foxnews.com/sports/nascars-new-charters-create-puzzling-payouts-for-drivers.print
- Maximum 4 charters per organization. Confidence: medium. https://speedwaydigest.com/index.php/news/regional-racing-news/890911-how-nascar-teams-make-money-and-why-charters-hold-so-much-value-today/

### 2.2 What a charter pays
- **2016-2024:** ~**$9M** guaranteed per chartered car per year (trial testimony). Confidence: high. https://www.nbcsports.com/nascar/news/denny-hamlin-testifies-that-signing-nascars-charter-deal-would-have-been-a-death-certificate
  - Press reports put the average at $8-10M per car (Heavy, 2024). Confidence: medium. https://heavy.com/sports/nascar/media-rights-revenue-nascar/
  - The last-place charter got about $4-5M a year. This was reported by Bob Pockrass and appears only in search summaries. Confidence: medium-low.
- **2025-2031 agreement:**

  - In late 2008 Chrysler went from ~$50M toward ~$30M (more than a 30% cut), while GM and Ford each cut ~20% (Reuters via NBC News, 2008-12-18).
  - Chrysler kept its contracts with 3 teams and dropped a 4th.
  - Confidence: medium. https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna28298654
- **Toyota's entry (2007):** an investment of **$100-200M** (NBC News, 2007-02-15). Its budget put pressure on the existing teams of the Big Three. Confidence: medium. https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna17114919

  - The OEMs never disclose per-team amounts (NASCAR.com 2015, search summary). Confidence: medium. https://www.nascar.com/news-media/2015/06/18/what-would-manufacturer-change-do-for-furniture-row/
  - Support is tiered: (1) factory or "flagship" teams get cash plus engineering, wind tunnel and simulator time; (2) alliance teams buy chassis, setups and data from the flagship (e.g. Furniture Row and Leavine with JGR on Toyota); (3) customer teams only lease engines.
  - Engines come from approved builders: Hendrick/ECR (Chevrolet), Roush Yates (Ford) and TRD (Toyota).
- **Engine leases:**
  - In 2010 Hendrick supplied SHR's engines at about **$4M per car** per year, a fixed cost (Forbes, search summary). Confidence: low. https://www.jayski.com/2010/03/25/hendrick-motorsports-still-deemed-most-vaulable/

  - SHR was due to lose some Ford support after 2024 (section 9).
  - In 2009 GM cut its support in the Nationwide and Truck series (Jayski 2009, search summary). Confidence: low-medium. https://www.jayski.com/2009/06/12/gm-cutting-support-in-nationwide-truck-series-update-2/

| Hendrick Motorsports | 2022 | Said it would not profit in 2022 despite back-to-back titles | https://heavy.com/sports/nascar/media-rights-revenue-nascar/ - medium |

**Common pattern (synthesis).** These are the triggers, in order of frequency:
1. A primary sponsor leaves with no replacement (MWR, FRR, Germain, SHR).
2. Fixed alliance, engine or car costs rise faster than revenue (FRR; the Next Gen car costs).
3. The owner loses interest or will not fund the losses (BKR, FRR, SHR).
4. Debt or tax default (BK Racing).

The exit route is usually **selling the charter**, which since 2022 is the team's most valuable asset.

**"Cheap" vs "competitive" (owner and practitioner statements)**
- **Cup:** competitive ≈ **$20M per car** (Hamlin and Jenkins, 2025). The lowest-revenue organization took in $8.2M per car in 2024, which is the effective "cheap" floor for a chartered car that loses money (Fox). An open car earns ~$2.9M a year in purse.
- **Xfinity:** ≈ **$4M** is the sustainable mid-pack minimum (2025). A competitive car ≈ **$5.5-6M** (Deegan, Autopian).
- **Trucks:** $3.2M actual vs ~$2M "should be" (Busch, 2017); $4.5M (Keselowski, 2017).
- **ARCA:** ~$0.2M independent vs $1M+ top team (low confidence).
- **Dirt late model national tour:** $200-250k for an established team adding a car vs $500-550k from scratch.
- **WoO sprint:** ~$620k a year for a top team (2022).

## 10. Suggested game parameters

Every row here is **EST**, derived from the sections above. Low-medium confidence.

- **Cup per-car budget, nominal:** use the table in section 1.1. Back-marker / mid / top multipliers ≈ 0.6 / 0.85 / 1.2 of the era midpoint.
- **Revenue mix for a Cup car:**

| Era | Sponsorship | Purse/charter | Merch/other | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016-2024 | 60-70% | 25-35% | ≤5% | e.g. $9M charter ÷ ~$20-25M revenue ≈ 35-45% for a mid team; less for top teams with $40M revenue |
| 2025+ | 50-60% | 35-45% | - | charter rises to $12.5M |

- **Pre-2001 TV money:** tracks sold rights individually, so team TV income was only via purses. No figure was found; model it as a purse-only share.
- **Era scaling, cost-of-racing index (nominal):**
  - Late Model Stock: ×3.2 from 1995 to 2018 (sourced).
  - Cup per car: about ×2-2.5 from the late 1990s ($8-10M competitive) to 2024 ($20M).
  - Use ×2.5 ± 0.5 for 1995 to 2025 at the pro tiers and ×3 at local and regional tiers.
- **Merch:** the driver's take is ~3% of gross trackside (2015+), and the top driver holds about 25-33% of the driver-merch market.
- **Popularity metric:** scale Cup's TV audience by era: 2005 ≈ 1.0 → 2010 ≈ 0.75 → 2015 ≈ 0.65 → 2021 ≈ 0.37 → 2025 ≈ 0.31. These use ~8M as the 2005 base (low confidence, see section 8); 2010 = 6.0/8, 2015 = 5.1/8, 2021 = 2.93/8, 2025 = 2.48/8.
